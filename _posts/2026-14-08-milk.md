Yesterday, I went to a near-bankrupt supermarket in my hometown. Of course, this is a great opportunity for someone thrifty (I prefer to call it financially selective) like me to go for a discount hunting. The first thing I look up is milk. I really love milk. Apparently, the time it took me to grab a single box of milk was much longer than I expected. The milk was placed in the back of the whole store.

Turns out, there is a reason you (I mean we) rarely find milk near the entrance of a supermarket.

At first, this sounds like a conspiracy theory invented by someone who has spent too much time shopping. But it is actually a rather sensible marketing strategy.

Milk is a *destination product*. People usually enter the supermarket already knowing they need it. So, if the milk is placed at the back, you have to walk past almost everything else before you get there.

And this is where things get interesting.

While walking to get your milk, you encounter snacks, drinks, promotions, seasonal products, and approximately seventeen things you did not know existed five minutes ago. Some of them might end up in your basket.

This is related to what researchers call **impulse buying**: purchases made with relatively little planning and often triggered by the shopping environment (*Beatty & Ferrell*, 1998). The supermarket does not necessarily need to convince you that you need chocolate. It just needs to make sure chocolate is there when you are already in the mood to buy something.

Putting milk at the back is not some magical formula that makes everyone buy more. Consumer behavior is complicated, annoyingly so. Shopping goals, time pressure, store layout, product placement, and individual differences can all influence what happens both inside and outside of a consumer’s mind. 

Which is probably the most interesting part of marketing (*smirk).

For a retailer, a supermarket is not simply a place where products wait for customers to adopt them. It is a place designed to tempt customers straight to the *“wall of purchase”*.

So next time you walk all the way to the back of the supermarket for milk, remember:

You came for one thing.

The supermarket (always) had other plans.
